# Paper learning

Paper learning should turn a source into a model the learner can reconstruct, not a summary they can reread.

## Argument structure

Prefer:

    problem
    → why it matters
    → limitation / gap
    → core claim
    → method / mechanism
    → evidence for the claim
    → what the evidence does not establish
    → assumptions / limitations
    → relation to other ideas

Do not blindly follow section order when dependency order is more useful.

## Two useful modes

### Follow source

Use when the learner explicitly wants to stay close to the paper or course order.

### Understanding first

Reorder by prerequisites and explanatory value when the learner's goal is independent understanding.

## Prerequisites are hypotheses

A concept that appears necessary is a routing hypothesis, not evidence the learner lacks it. Use self-report to skip obvious redundancy and verify only when the distinction changes teaching.

## Source fidelity

Distinguish:

- what the source actually says;
- your explanation;
- external context;
- uncertain interpretation.

Use research/search when a claim or citation materially needs verification. Do not pretend an opaque source was read.

## Active paper learning

Useful learner moves include:

- state the research problem without jargon;
- reconstruct why prior approaches are insufficient;
- explain one mechanism from memory;
- map claim → evidence;
- identify what a result does not prove;
- predict what changes if an assumption is removed;
- compare with a neighboring method;
- transfer the argument pattern to another paper or problem.

## Completion

Reading or summarizing is not completion.

For an important paper, stronger evidence includes independent argument reconstruction, evidence/limitation reasoning, delayed retrieval, and transfer/comparison.
